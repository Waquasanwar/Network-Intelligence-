The difference

A database of 40,000 CVs you have never met. Eleven people who will put their name to someone.

Recruitment starts with a search. A referral network starts with a question somebody can actually answer: who do we genuinely know who could solve this?

So we do not sell you a shortlist. We tell you who in our network has worked with this person, what they saw them do, and whether they would work with them again — and then we ask the person before we tell you their name.

The sequence

How an introduction actually happens

  1. 01

    You tell us the role in plain words

    “Programme director for a core banking recovery, Dubai, starting January, needs to already have the right to work here.” No job spec, no competency matrix. We read it back to you before anything moves.

    We confirm what we assumed, so nothing is invented.

  2. 02

    We look in the network, not on the internet

    We check who we have worked with, who is on the Amana bench, who somebody here has vouched for by name, and who is genuinely available right now — not who updated a profile eighteen months ago.

    Hard requirements are checked first: location, right to work, route, rate.

  3. 03

    You see an anonymised card

    Capability, region, availability, rate band, right-to-work status and how well we know them. Never a name, never an employer, never a contact detail — because they have not agreed to this role yet.

    This is the card below. It is the real thing.

  4. 04

    We ask them, and only then do you get a name

    We go back to the person, describe your role, and ask. If they say yes, the card unlocks: name, history, the evidence behind the trust score, and a conversation in the diary. If they say no, you never learn who they were.

    A fee is due only when someone actually starts.

The score

Trust is measured, not claimed

A single number hides what it is made of, so we show the four parts separately. A match built on capability alone means something quite different from one built on trust.

  • Capability80 / 100

    We have used them ourselves, and four people here vouch by name.

  • Practical74 / 100

    Right to work is met; start date still to confirm.

  • Style62 / 100

    Direct with stakeholders — a strength in a recovery, a risk in a consensus culture.

And the honest part. A requirement that is genuinely unmet caps the score at 44, however brilliant the person. No amount of capability fixes a visa, and we would rather tell you that on day one than on the day they cannot start.

Consent

Nobody is in here without knowing

We hold things people told us in confidence. So the permissions are granular, withdrawal is immediate, and every class of data has a date on which it is deleted or anonymised.

  • Shown anonymously, or not at all

    One switch. Turn it off and they disappear from every client and agency view immediately — including cards already on a shortlist.

  • The name is released per role, never in general

    We ask about your role specifically. Consent to one introduction is not consent to the next one.

  • Photos are optional, and off by default

    Most profiles here have no photo at all. Initials, everywhere, unless someone chooses otherwise.

  • Your answers are never sent to a speech vendor

    The interviewer can speak our questions aloud. What you say becomes text on your own device; no audio is uploaded or stored.

Built to the stricter reading of both regimes that cover this network — UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018, and UAE Federal Decree-Law No. 45 of 2021, the PDPL, which is consent-first and requires withdrawal to be as easy as consent was to give. Profile and conversation data: 24 months from last contact. Fee and audit records: kept as long as the law requires, then anonymised.
